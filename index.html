Blind Eye

Personal project, 2025

Smart glasses that narrate the wearer's surroundings

A wearable prototype that uses computer vision to identify objects around a visually impaired user and describe them out loud.

Watch the demo video
Goal
Build glasses that could be worn anywhere and describe the wearer's surroundings in real time.
How it works
A Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 on the frame hosts a web server with a live video feed. A laptop pulls that stream and runs Ultralytics YOLO object detection with custom Python code to pick out relevant objects and their positions, which are then narrated to the user.
Result
The detection and narration pipeline worked as intended. In a test walking through random indoor and outdoor areas, 92 of 100 recorded detections were correct (92% accuracy). With a three-month build window, the glasses still depend on a Wi-Fi connection, so they can't yet be used just anywhere. A second version aimed at closing that gap is in progress.

NASA RASC-AL Lunar Rover

In progress, 2026–present

Power & Thermal Team Lead, leading the power, thermal, and communications sub-team

Our team is competing in NASA's RASC-AL 2027 competition under Theme 1, Enabling Extreme Exploration: a rover for long-duration, autonomous traverses through the Moon's harshest terrain. We're designing for the high-altitude ridge traverse, targeting Malapert Mountain or Leibnitz Beta near the lunar south pole. I lead the sub-team that sizes the power system, keeps the rover within its operating temperatures, and plans how it communicates with Earth.

Primary constraints: the rover has to survive lunar cold below 40 K and go long stretches with zero sunlight while staying self-sufficient, with no other structures or power plants to lean on. It also has to be rugged enough to explore every extreme environment the Moon can throw at it and still carry out demanding science and exploration missions.
Design decisions so far
AreaWhat we chose
Baseline powereMMRTG, Pu-238, 145 W continuous
Keeps the rover powered through shadowed regions and lunar night, independent of sunlight.
Solar1.5 m² flat panel, dual-axis tracking
Chosen over a single-axis mount and a dome-shaped array because south-pole sites need extreme panel tilt to track a sun that stays near the horizon.
Energy storage24 kWh lithium-ion battery bank
Sized so that, from a full charge and with the eMMRTG, the rover can run about 3 days with zero sunlight.
Power budget500 W in sunlight
Set with a fully charged battery, leaving a safety margin.
ThermalPassive first
Insulation plus eMMRTG waste heat hold the operating temperature, with heaters only as a backup.
CommunicationsHybrid RF + optical (under evaluation)
Always-on RF for reliability and laser links for bandwidth, relayed through a satellite when the rover is out of direct view of Earth.

Automated Pet Feeder

Personal project, 2024

Food and water dispenser controlled from any device on the network

A web-connected feeder that shows how full each bowl is and refills food or water on command.

Goal
Wirelessly open an interface, see what's in the bowls, and choose when to dispense more food or water.
How it works
An ESP32 hosts a web server showing jug and bowl levels with a refill button for each bowl. A water pump handles water, a stepper motor turns a 3D-printed auger for food, and load cells under the bowls report how full they are.
Result
A working prototype, with the full enclosure designed in CAD.
